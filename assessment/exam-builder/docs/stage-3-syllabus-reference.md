## Content, by focus area

### Represents numbers A — MA3-RN-01/02/03
- Whole numbers: recognise, represent and order numbers in the millions —
  name millions using ones/tens/hundreds grouping; ascending and descending
  order using place value; round to a specified place value
- Whole numbers: apply place value to partition, regroup and rename to 1 billion —
  1000 thousands = 1 million, 1000 millions = 1 billion; regroup in different
  forms; partition in non-standard forms
- Decimals and percentages: extend the place value system beyond thousandths
- Decimals and percentages: compare, order and represent decimals

### Represents numbers B — MA3-RN-01/02/03
- Whole numbers: locate and represent integers on a number line
- Decimals and percentages: connect benchmark fractions, decimals and percentages
- Decimals and percentages: determine percentage discounts of 10%, 25% and 50%

### Additive relations A — MA3-AR-01
- Efficient mental and written strategies for addition and subtraction —
  word problems including multistep; levelling, addition for subtraction,
  constant difference, bridging; place value to add or subtract 3+ numbers with
  different digit counts; when a calculator is more efficient; identify
  efficient vs inefficient multidigit subtraction strategies
- Estimation and place value to judge reasonableness of answers

### Additive relations B — MA3-AR-01
- Choose and use efficient strategies for addition and subtraction
- Apply known strategies to add and subtract decimals

### Multiplicative relations A — MA3-MR-01/02
- Determine products and factors
- Partitioning and place value to multiply 2-, 3- and 4-digit numbers by a one-digit number
- Mental and written strategies to multiply 2- and 3-digit by 2-digit numbers
- Represent and solve division problems with whole number remainders
- Divide a number with 3+ digits by a one-digit divisor
- Estimation and rounding to check reasonableness

### Multiplicative relations B — MA3-MR-01/02
- Multiplication and division with larger numbers
- Multiply and divide decimals by powers of 10
- Equivalent number sentences involving × and ÷ to find unknown quantities
- Represent and describe number patterns formed by multiples
- Brackets and the order of operations in number sentences

### Representing quantity fractions A — MA3-RQF-01/02
- The number 1 as the whole
- Compare and order common unit fractions
- Add and subtract fractions with the same denominator

### Representing quantity fractions B — MA3-RQF-01/02
- A fraction can represent a division
- Compare common fractions with related denominators
- Build up to the whole from a given fractional part
- Use equivalence to add and subtract fractional quantities
- Fractional quantities of whole numbers (halves, quarters, fifths, tenths)

### Geometric measure A — MA3-GM-01/02/03
- Position: the Cartesian coordinate system — grid-map reference gives an area
  while the number plane identifies a point; lines are numbered, not spaces;
  origin (0, 0); plot and label points in the first quadrant, horizontal first;
  record coordinates of given points
- Length: metres and kilometres; 1000 m = 1 km
- Length: measure lengths to find perimeters
- Angles: estimate, measure and compare angles in degrees
- Angles: use a protractor to measure and identify types of angles

### Geometric measure B — MA3-GM-01/02/03
- Position: all 4 quadrants of the coordinate plane
- Length: connect decimal representations to the metric system
- Length: convert between common metric units
- Length: compare lengths using appropriate units
- Angles: angles on a straight line and angles at a point
- Angles: relationships formed by intersecting straight lines

### Two-dimensional spatial structure A — MA3-2DS-01/03
- 2D shapes: classify shapes and describe properties
- Area: hectares and square kilometres
- Area: calculate areas of rectangles in familiar metric units

### Two-dimensional spatial structure B — MA3-2DS-01/02/03
- 2D shapes: dissect and rearrange using translations, reflections, rotations
- Area: composite figures
- Area: parallelogram by subdivision and rearrangement
- Area: triangle

### Three-dimensional spatial structure A — MA3-3DS-01/02
- 3D objects: compare, describe and name prisms and pyramids
- 3D objects: connect 3D objects with 2D representations
- Volume: appropriate units for capacity
- Volume: displacement to investigate volumes of irregular solids
- Volume: connect decimal representations to the metric system

### Three-dimensional spatial structure B — MA3-3DS-01/02
- 3D objects: construct prisms and pyramids
- Volume: cubic metres
- Volume: the multiplicative structure for finding volume
- Volume: volumes of rectangular prisms in cm³ and m³

### Non-spatial measure A — MA3-NSM-01/02
- Mass: appropriate units for mass
- Mass: connect decimal representations to the metric system
- Time: compare 12- and 24-hour systems and convert between them

### Non-spatial measure B — MA3-NSM-01/02
- Mass: convert between common metric units of mass
- Time: problems involving duration, using 12- and 24-hour time

## Calibration — settled on the first slice, apply to the rest

These came out of building **Represents Numbers** and should hold across the
Stage 3 banks unless there is a reason to depart.

- **Whole numbers** reach the millions. The billion boundary appears only as a
  naming or regrouping fact, never as arithmetic.
- **Decimals** stop at 3 places, per MA3-RN-02.
- **Percentages** are restricted to the benchmark set 10%, 25%, 50%, plus 20%,
  75% and 100% where they fall out of the benchmark fractions.
- **Quantities are chosen so every answer is exact.** Amounts are multiples of
  20 so 10%, 25% and 50% are all whole numbers, and money never needs rounding.
- **Marks: one for a single step, two for anything needing working**, three for
  multi-part. This is what drives the difficulty ramp
  (`orderQuestionsForPractice`) and, with the answer length, the worksheet
  answer space.
- **A one-mark question does not always get a box.** Marks say whether working
  is expected; the `answer` string says whether a 22mm box can hold it. Over
  12 characters, or a prompt asking the student to explain, gets ruled lines
  instead. Found on the first Stage 3 worksheet, where "Write 51 821 724 in
  words" and "Write these decimals in order: …" were both given a box. This is
  universal — it applies to the Stage 4 and 5 banks too.
- **Negatives use a typographic minus (−), not a hyphen.** Also found on that
  first worksheet: "Place -11 on the number line".
- **Money spaces its thousands** like every other number on the page:
  `$5 320.00`, not `$5320.00`.
- **Decimal answers keep the place value they were asked in** — 29.9 − 21.9 is
  8.0, not 8.
- **Fixed-form question types need generated variety.** A type built from a
  short list of hard-coded forms repeats verbatim as soon as a paper asks for
  more than a handful; generate the parameters instead.
- **A stacked fraction prints about three characters wide**, so a list of them
  needs ruled lines even though the token text is short. `resolveAnswerSpace`
  counts fraction tokens at their printed width for this reason.
- **An ordering question must not be born already ordered** — reshuffle until
  the displayed order differs from the answer.
- **Watch singular/plural in generated word problems** ("1 sandwiches").
- **A shape diagram must carry its dimensions.** The area engine draws every
  rectangle at the same fixed proportions, so an unlabelled figure beside a
  prompt stating "9 m by 7 m" silently contradicts it. Pass `lengthLabel` /
  `widthLabel` / `baseLabel` / `heightLabel` and set `notToScale: true`, then
  let the prompt just ask for the area.
- **Units belong in the answer** wherever the outcome mentions choosing them —
  MA3-2DS-03 does, so every area answer carries cm², m², hectares or km².
- **Multi-part questions** carry a summary `answer` for the key and empty
  top-level `working`; the parts hold their own working. Matches Stage 4.
- **Place-value tables cap at 7 columns.** One column per digit, and a wider
  table cannot fit a textbook column with its headings legible.
- **Distractor-free wording.** Prompts avoid the "A student wrote… this is
  incorrect" framing used in Stage 4 error-spotting; at Stage 3 it invites the
  misconception to be remembered.
- **A misconception the syllabus states outright gets its own question type.**
  Geometric measure names two: a grid reference gives an *area* while a
  coordinate gives a *point*, and *the lines are numbered, not the spaces*.
  Both are content in their own right, so both are asked directly rather than
  assumed inside a plotting question.
- **The number plane is drawn one square larger than the largest coordinate in
  play.** A point sitting on the frame collides with the axis numbering and is
  hard to read, so a plane with `xMax: 6` only ever carries points up to 5.
- **A question answered ON the diagram gets no answer space** — `space: "none"`.
  "Plot the point A (3, 4)" is finished on the grid, and a ruled line beneath it
  is wasted paper. Same rule as the worksheet rebuild.
- **Angles a student must measure are multiples of 5°.** That is the finest
  reading a classroom protractor supports, and every drawn angle is at least
  25° so its arc can be labelled.
- **Stage boundaries are enforced, not assumed.** The angle engine can draw
  vertically opposite and parallel-line diagrams; those relationships are Stage
  4, so the Stage 3 bank never calls them and its harness asserts as much. The
  same goes for language — no "alternate", "co-interior" or "corresponding".
- **A perimeter answer is a length, an area answer is squared.** Worth checking
  explicitly in the harness, since the two live in adjacent topics.

Every bank gets its own harness in `tools/` (`node tools/stage3-<topic>.mjs`,
plain Node, no dependencies). A harness re-derives every answer *independently
of the bank* — reading the dimensions off the diagram rather than the prompt
where it can, so the figure and the answer are checked against each other — and
then asserts the scope boundary and the answer-space resolution. Run
`tools/verify.mjs` afterwards for the schema, diagram-render and token-leak
sweep across every bank.
